The Claremont Colleges

Seven colleges, one square mile

The Village wraps around one of the most unusual academic communities in the country — five undergraduate colleges and two graduate schools sharing a single campus smaller than many city parks.

✿

⭐ Featured College

Harvey Mudd College

Harvey Mudd was founded in 1955 at a very particular moment in American anxiety. The Cold War was escalating, the Soviet Union was pulling ahead in the space race, and the US was suddenly, urgently aware that it didn't have enough engineers and scientists. Congress would pass the National Defense Education Act in 1958; Harvey Mudd opened three years earlier with the same panic driving it — a private answer to a national problem.

But its founders added an unusual twist: they insisted that engineers who couldn't communicate, reason ethically, or understand history were a danger as much as a resource. So one-third of every student's coursework is permanently devoted to the humanities and social sciences. The college was named for Harvey Seeley Mudd, a mining magnate who helped fund it — though he died before it ever opened its doors.

On clear days, students can see the Pacific Ocean from the north end of campus. Despite having fewer than 1,000 students, HMC produces more PhD graduates per capita than virtually any other undergraduate institution in the country.

🎭 The Caltech Prank Wars

Harvey Mudd and Caltech — 35 miles apart — have maintained one of the great inter-college prank rivalries in American history. Mudders have stolen Caltech's cannon (twice), hacked their scoreboard at the Rose Bowl to read "CALTECH" during a USC game, and on one occasion reassembled a car on the roof of a campus building. Caltech has returned in kind. The pranks are elaborate, technically impressive, and governed by an unspoken code: no permanent damage, no one gets hurt, and the better prank wins. Faculty tend to look the other way.

Campus Life · Open to All

Walk the Campus

Harvey Mudd's campus is compact, beautiful, and open to visitors. The architecture is mid-century modern, and the mountain views from the north end of campus are some of the best in the region. Walk freely and soak it in.

↗ Open in Maps
Shared Resource · All 7 Colleges

The Claremont Colleges Library

All seven colleges share a single library system with over two million volumes. Students can cross-enroll in classes at any college, eat at any dining hall, and access five swimming pools and four fitness centers — a remarkable collegiate ecosystem.

Nature & Outdoors

Where the foothills begin

Claremont sits at the base of the San Gabriel Mountains. You're never more than a few minutes from a trail or a garden.

✿

California Botanic Garden

The largest garden in the US dedicated entirely to California native plants. On overcast days, low-hanging mountain mist drifts through the arroyos and makes it feel otherworldly. Squirrels, turtles, lizards, rabbits. $15 admission.

🕐 Tue–Sun 8 am – 8 pm (Closed Monday) ★ 4.7  1500 N College Ave

Claremont Hills Wilderness Park

2,500+ acres of open chaparral in the San Gabriel foothills with sweeping views — on clear days, all the way to the Pacific. Trails range from flat fire roads to steep single-track. Free, dawn to dusk.

Local Tips

A few things to know before you go

✿
Getting Here

Parking & Transit

Street parking on 1st St and surrounding blocks is generally free and easy on weekdays. Weekends, use the free lot behind the Packing House. The Metrolink San Bernardino Line stops right at the Depot — a scenic, stress-free ride from LA Union Station.

Best Times

When to Visit

Sunday mornings are magical — the farmers market runs 8 am to 1 pm, shops open at 10, and the botanic garden is at its best in cool morning light. October through April the air is crisp and the mountains are often snow-capped.

College Town Energy

Student Discounts

Many Village shops and restaurants offer discounts to Claremont Colleges students — always worth asking. Campus buildings are beautiful and generally open to walkers. Check the colleges' event calendars for free lectures, performances, and gallery openings.
